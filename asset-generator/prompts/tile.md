Seamless tiling (most important requirement): this image will be repeated edge to edge in a grid, so it must wrap perfectly in both directions. The pattern running off the right edge must continue exactly where the left edge begins, and the pattern running off the bottom edge must continue exactly where the top edge begins. Do not draw a border, a frame, a vignette, a mat, or any edge treatment. Do not center a motif in the middle of the square — the pattern must be even across the whole image so no single tile stands out once repeated. Do not let any feature touch an edge in a way that would be cut in half without its other half appearing on the opposite edge.

Where to put the joints: if the material has grout lines, panel seams, plank joints or tile edges, arrange them so they fall INSIDE the image — for example a cross of seams through the middle — and leave the four outer edges as plain uninterrupted field. Do not run a seam along an edge of the image and do not draw a half-width seam at an edge. An edge that is plain field on both sides is what makes the wrap invisible; once repeated, the interior seams read as a regular grid anyway.

Camera: straight top-down, perfectly flat, no perspective, no horizon, no vanishing point. The surface is seen face-on as pure texture.

Lighting: perfectly even and flat across the whole image. No light source, no gradient from one side to the other, no highlight, no bloom, no vignette darkening at the corners — uneven lighting is the fastest way to make a repeated tile show its grid.

Style: flat, graphic and low-detail, the way a 16-bit game floor is drawn. The material reads through a few large flat shapes and hard-edged seams, not through fine grain. Keep the overall value range narrow so characters and props stay readable on top of it, but keep the seams and joints clearly visible so the surface does not read as an empty color field.
